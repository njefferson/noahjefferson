Walk a plan

One page for thinking a plan through before anything goes into a tool. Work down it in order; every box takes plain lines. At the bottom it hands you two things: a file for Quietkeep carrying only what should come back to you, and the document that holds the rest of the thinking. A draft stays in this browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

1The mission, in one sentence

Who, what, when, where, why. This line names the project everything else sits under.

2Intent — so people can act without asking you

Purpose, the few tasks that matter most, and what done looks like. This travels into Quietkeep as the project’s note, and it is the piece most plans skip.

3Facts stays in the document

What is true and checkable, one per line. A fact does not come back to you, so it does not cross — it anchors the document.

4Assumptions — each becomes a thing to confirm

One per line. Add -> and what would turn it into a fact. Each line crosses as a Confirm: task, because an assumption nobody re-checks stopped being an assumption — untick any you would not spend a task on.

5Constraints and restraints stays in the document

Must-dos and must-nots, one per line. Statements, not returns — they stay with the document.

6Tasks — specified, implied, essential

One per line in each box. Dates ride along as @due(2026-12-01). Name who holds a piece in the line itself — names survive as words, and you link the person on the item’s own sheet afterwards, because an import never creates a person link. Essential tasks arrive hot — interest, never a rank.

7What must reach you at once

Write each as If … -> then … — the trigger, then what you will do or decide. Phrasing a trigger with its action attached is the single best-evidenced move in the planning literature, and it crosses as a task that carries both halves.

8Decision points

One per line: decision @by(2026-11-15) @when(what makes it live). The @by date is the last moment the decision keeps all its options, and it crosses as the due date. No date — it still crosses, just undated.

9Premortem — it failed; why?

It is months from now and this plan quietly failed. One cause per line, and after -> what would have headed it off. Lines with a heading-off cross as tasks; causes you cannot act on stay in the document as named risks.

For Quietkeep — choose what crosses

Everything starts on. Untick a line and it stays out of the file and out of the clipboard — it is never created, so there is nothing to delete later. Editing a line switches it back on.

Then, in Quietkeep: the ⓘ panel → Getting things in from somewhere else → choose the file → Bring it in. Bringing in adds — it removes nothing that is already there, and the same file twice adds nothing twice.

The document — everything, including what does not cross

Facts, constraints, unactioned risks, and the plan itself. Keep it wherever you keep documents; it is the half you re-read when an assumption needs revisiting.

What does not cross, and why

Facts, constraints and restraints, and causes with no heading-off action: statements, not returns — Quietkeep only holds what has to come back. People: names survive inside the task words, but no import creates a person link — open each item’s sheet and link them there. Standing conditions you mean to watch: give the thing itself It runs itself in Quietkeep and write the condition under What would change it? — a file cannot set that field.

Where this walk comes from. The order is mission analysis as military planning doctrine teaches it — facts before assumptions, implied tasks as their own category, intent written for people out of contact. The doctrine is an institutional practice, not a controlled finding. Two steps carry direct research: if–then phrasing is implementation intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 69–119 — a large meta-analysis), and the premortem builds on prospective hindsight (Mitchell, Russo & Pennington, 1989, Journal of Behavioral Decision Making 2, 25–38). Citations checked against the publisher on 2026-09-01; effect sizes deliberately not quoted here.
