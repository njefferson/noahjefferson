# The program

This file ranks every item of work across the hub and the apps. A plan serves one item, quotes its acceptance, and at its close writes the item's state and what changed. New work enters by being ranked here, and the next item taken is the highest in rank that no open dependency blocks.

## Thesis

Every app in the family is free, runs on the reader's own device, works offline after its first load, asks for no account, collects no analytics, keeps no user data on a server, and draws only on honest or open data sources. A change that gives one of these up changes what the app is, and is named before it is made. A build that trades one of them away is a separate product under its own name with its own honest claim, and the default build never weakens to make room for it. The hub is the owner's personal link page for the photography accounts and these free web apps, static and self-contained with no build step, and it is the home of the doctrine, the lessons, the palettes and the gates every app shares. The sessions that build the apps work for the owner, whose part is judgement: approving a plan, reading a result, settling a choice only the owner can settle. Each app keeps its own thesis in its own notes, and the wall question below reads that thesis before it reads anything else.

## How an item moves

### The six states

- open: ranked and waiting. No plan carries it.
- planned: a plan that carries it is written and waits for the owner's button.
- running: an approved plan that carries it is in force.
- parked: it waits on a choice or a setting only the owner can make. The entry names what it waits on and what it unblocks, and the session takes the next item that does not depend on it.
- done: its acceptance was observed. The entry says what was observed and which plan's close recorded it.
- ejected: a plan that carried it closed unfinished. The entry keeps why, the finding that moved it and the dependencies the plan moved, the item is re-ranked, and a plan takes it up only after reading that entry.

An item moves from open to planned to running to done. Any of open, planned or running moves to parked when it comes to wait on the owner, and back to open once the owner has settled it. Running moves to ejected when its plan ejects.

### The three ejects

- A step ejects to the plan. It stops, writes what it saw and the files it names, and the plan is rewritten from those files.
- A plan ejects to the program. It closes unfinished, its item goes back to the list as ejected with the finding attached and is re-ranked, the dependencies it moved are marked, and the next item that does not depend on it is taken.
- The program ejects to the owner. This is only for a choice that is the owner's: a goal, a thesis, a taste, or a trade between two items only the owner can rank. It is put once, with the items it blocks named, that item is parked, and the session takes the next item that does not depend on it.

Each eject is a recorded move with its reason, so a later plan cannot pick the item up without reading why a plan stopped.

### The wall question

One question is answered in writing before a step goes, at every stop inside it, and at a plan's close. It has three parts: does this still serve the item's acceptance, does the item still serve the thesis, and did what was just seen move either. It is read upward and is answered from this file and the thesis, not from the plan, because the plan is the ladder and the wall is what the ladder leans on. A no is an eject. A no on the first part ejects the step to the plan, a no on the second ejects the plan to the program, and a move of a goal, a thesis, a taste or a trade between items ejects the program to the owner.

### Length

No count of lines or characters gates a plan or a design, or shortens one. A plan is as long as what it requires, and so is a design.

### The form of an entry

An entry has a key of the form P-1 and a title, then its repository, goal, state and plan, then its acceptance, which is what is observed when it is done, and its dependencies, which are item keys, queue keys, settings only the owner can change, and pins. The goal is the number of one of these six goals, which are the standing goals of the instruction file and the redesign's own goal 1.

- Goal 1: sessions work the owner's way by structure and not by a model's memory.
- Goal 2: the owner's time and credit go to judgement only.
- Goal 3: the work is visible every few minutes without being asked for.
- Goal 4: only a choice the owner alone can make comes back to the owner.
- Goal 5: an approved plan runs to its end, and what is found on the way is handled and recorded for the next plan.
- Goal 6: the owner's rules bind as written, held by gates that leave the main thread no choice.

The plan field names the plan that carries the item, or none. The redesign plan is the plan of 2 November 2026 whose steps are 50 to 62; it wrote this file.

### Rank

The list is the rank. The items the redesign plan builds come first, then the parked settings, then the harness items (the blind areas and the documented mechanisms), then the knowledge layers, then the dependency updates and the sibling pins, then the findings of the Found queue, in the order the audit of 1 November 2026 ranks them where it names them and by key otherwise.
