## 366 · A whole-frame read of a full-size export is a thumbnail, because the image reader fits everything to 2000 pixels

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST tile-at-reader-limit — before any claim about detail in an image wider or taller than 2000 px, cut it into tiles no larger than 2000 px on the long edge, open every tile, and cite the tile each claim came from; if the reader may have changed, measure its limit first with a 1-pixel grating card.

**Smell:** a sentence about grain, fringing, a halo or an edge in a 5600-pixel export, written after opening the export once.

**Measured 2026-09-25 in Jefferson-Photography-Studio.** Three looks were
compared on two photographs, six exports of 5600 x 3728. The comparison had
been made from whole-frame reads and reduced sheets, and it had produced
claims: a light halo round the pylons under one look, orange grass and ringed
cladding under another. Then the reader was measured. Test cards carrying a
1-pixel black and white grating read crisply at 1400, 1600 and 2000 pixels
wide. At 2800 the grating came back as flat grey, and the reader reported the
card displayed at 2000 x 1331. So a 5600-pixel export reaches the session at
0.36 of its size, and every whole-frame read before that point had been a
thumbnail about a third of full size.

Opening all 96 tiles at 1:1 (16 positions, three looks, two frames) confirmed
the three claims, and it found things no reduced view had shown. Both tuned
looks clipped the lit grass to flat white over a fifth of a tile, about nine
times the shipped look. One look darkened a pale building face as if it were
sky, with a bright round hole in the darkening. A faint vertical colour step
sat at the same x on both photographs, which puts it in the pipeline rather
than the scene. And two of three edges seen by eye in smooth sky measured as
nothing, a ramp of a few levels with no step. At 1:1 an edge in a gradient is
still a reason to measure, not yet a finding.

**Why it happens.** A whole scene looks sharp at any size, so nothing in the
reduced picture says it is reduced. The note naming the reduction arrives
beside the picture and is easy to read past. Lesson 365 covers pictures sent to
someone else too small to show a difference. This is the same defect on the
session's own side, and it comes first: the pictures that went out too small
were chosen from views that were already too small.

**The rule.** Tiles no larger than the reader's limit, which is 2000 pixels on
the long edge as measured here: nine tiles for a 5600 x 3728 frame, not the
sixteen this inspection used before the limit was known. Every tile opened,
every claim citing its tile, and an edge seen in a smooth gradient measured
before it is reported. A crop sent on keeps the same limit, so it is seen at
1:1 by both sides.
