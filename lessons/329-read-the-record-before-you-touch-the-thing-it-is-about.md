## 329 · Read the record before you touch the thing it is about — the answer is usually already written down, by you, in the file you have open

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST read-the-record-before-the-first-edit

Deliberately NOT declared a gate. No parser can tell reading from
having-read, and a check that claimed to would be the "appearance of a fix"
this family already has a lesson about. What a parser CAN do is put the
sentence in front of you unasked, twice:
`Jefferson-Photography-Studio:tools/session-brief.mjs` prints the top-ranked
item's chosen and rejected options at session start, and
`tools/decisions-check.mjs` prints them again on every commit.

**The owner's instruction, 2026-09-19: do not start new work without looking at
the research already gathered, or at least reading the problem as it has
already been written up.**

## What it cost

**The headline, because it is the one that should be impossible.** A decision
record was being implemented. Its **Rejected** section said, in writing, that
routing the effect through a hue BAND was wrong — *"a hue band is not a place.
Teals and blues wherever they are reaches water, shade with a blue cast and a
blue car"* — and noted that this had been the record's own first draft,
corrected within the hour. The session implementing that record then built a
measurement harness that drove the hue band, rendered three arms through it,
took every per-frame number off those renders, sent five comparison sheets to
the owner, and committed a value chosen from them. Two controls, named one line
apart in the same function, differing by a three-letter suffix. The record had
already answered which one was the subject. Nobody re-read it.

**And the same session did it again, four hours later, with research it had run
itself.** Asked about combining masks and told explicitly to follow standard
convention, it searched Lightroom and darktable, got a clean answer — a mask is
a GROUP of components joined by add / subtract / intersect, and the adjustment
belongs to the group — wrote that into a plan file, and then had to be told
twice ("look at what people actually do already", "look at your research
already gathered") before it used what it had just gathered.

## The rule

**Before touching a thing, read what has already been written about that thing,
and check the work against it.** Specifically, before the first edit:

- the decision record's **Options** — which one was CHOSEN, and by what name;
- its **Rejected** — and for each, what route it rules out, because a rejected
  route is usually the convenient one and is therefore the one a session
  reinvents;
- any research already gathered this session, including research in a plan file
  or a scratch note that nobody re-opened.

**A rejected option is not history, it is a live boundary.** It is written down
precisely because it looks reasonable; that is what makes it worth rejecting in
writing rather than merely not choosing.

## Why the mechanism is exposure and not refusal

A gate can compare two lists. It cannot compare a document against an
intention, and any check that claimed to would be the "appearance of a fix"
this family already has a lesson about. So the record is put in front of the
session twice instead — at start, generated from the ranked roadmap, and again
on every commit — and the rule is written where the standing rules live.

The honest limit: this one relies on reading. It is a CHECKLIST and it says so.
What raises the odds is that the sentence arrives unasked, in the two places a
session cannot avoid looking.
