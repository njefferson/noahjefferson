## 422 · A plan's review step could be edited until its text named no review file, which kept the approval and switched the review off for every later step, so the approval now records the review step and both gates refuse every send while none remains

**Enforced by:** GATE hub:session-guards.test.mjs — the cases that approve a plan through the tool and through the owner's exit in the app and read the review steps the marker records, amend the review step's text until it names no review file and read that the record stays as approved, read the list back through `approvedPlan`, send steps through the dispatch gate, the review gate and the hook itself against a plan whose review step has been edited away, and end a turn against a plan with a step added after approval, with plants in approved-plan-guard.mjs, hook-dispatch.mjs, report.mjs and stop-guard.mjs for every part whose removal must fail a case

**Recorded 2026-10-23.** Lesson 421 closed two routes past the owner's reading of a plan, a step added to the Steps and a step put on the Standing line, and named a third in its last paragraph without closing it. The review gate enforces only where a Standing step names the review file by its full path and says follow (`aimReviewSteps`). An edit of a step's text keeps the approval, by design, so that a refused route is answered by an amendment. An edit of the review step's text that dropped the path, or an edit that took the step off the Standing line, therefore left a plan the gates read as having no review step at all: the review gate returned at once for every step, the dispatch gate fell back on the pointer, and each later step went with no fresh agent reading its route. The Standing step itself was refused by the kind check, but the steps beside it were not. Nothing in the marker could tell a plan that never had a review from a plan whose review had been edited away, because the marker held a hash and the numbers of the Steps and of the Standing line, and the property that made a step a review lives in its words.

**What is done now.** The marker records, beside the Steps and the Standing numbers, the numbers of the plan's review steps (`review`, read by `approvedStepSet` in report.mjs), by the tool's approval and by the owner's exit in the app alike, and an amendment keeps it as approved. `reviewRecordProblem` in hook-dispatch.mjs is asked first by the dispatch gate and by the review gate, before any other judgement, and refuses every step, a Standing one included, while the marker records a review step and no Standing step of the plan any more names the review file by its full path and says follow. The refusal names the rule, lists the recorded numbers, says the plan goes back through plan mode, and sets no latch: the dispatch gate's carries the fence's no-latch mark and the review gate's never latched. A marker written before the record was made, and one that recorded an empty list because the plan had no review step, carry no such check. The stop guard now counts as a step left only a step whose number the marker recorded (`stepsLeft` in stop-guard.mjs), so a step added to the Order after approval, which the dispatch gate refuses to send, no longer holds every stop of the plan with no move that clears it; a marker with no step list counts as before. Doctrine rule 2 gains the clause that the review step's text keeps the review file's path, rule 14's list of refusals that set no latch gains this refusal, the dispatch gate's refusal of a step outside the recorded set and the two gates' refusal of a Standing step that was not on the line at approval or is of the wrong kind, with its count raised to match, and the hub CLAUDE.md latch bullet says the same in one sentence.

**The excuse is the one lesson 421 names.** An edit of the review step's text arrives with a reason, as the others did: the path reads as clutter, the step is long, the wording can be shorter. The reason is usually true as far as it goes. The test is whether the control was the owner's, and the review that clears each move was.

**What the check cannot see.** The record is of numbers and not of words. A recorded Standing step that is a route check, edited so that its text names the review file and says follow, is counted by the gate as a review step, and no gate compares the review step's text with what it was when approved. The review agent's instructions are the file the step points at, and the gates do not read that file's content. A plan approved before the record was made is judged as it was. The status page still prints `next: step N` for a step added to the Order after approval, because `pointerOf` in report.mjs reads the plan and not the marker, though the dispatch gate refuses to send it and the stop guard no longer counts it.

**The general form.** An exemption keyed on a property of a text, here that a step names a file, is a switch the text can turn off, and an approval that survives edits of the text turns it off without a word. Record at the moment of approval that the property held, and refuse every send once it no longer does, so that removing a safeguard costs the same as adding a step: a new plan and a new look.
