## 365 · A recommendation made from a picture too small to show the difference is a guess, and the next picture reverses it

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST closeup-before-recommending — before recommending between rendered candidates, open a close-up of each at full size or larger, two candidates side by side, at the places they differ; a recommendation cites that close-up and nothing smaller.

**Smell:** a recommendation followed within one exchange by "the close-up changes my recommendation".

**Measured 2026-09-25 in Jefferson-Photography-Studio.** Two looks were
compared on four-column sheets of whole frames, each column about a quarter
of an iPad's width, with a 1:1 sky crop per column reduced to the same width.
A recommendation went out for the second look, described as "a little more
textured" in the sky and with edge fringing "the same in both". The sheets
could not be read on the device. The close-up made next, two columns at full
size enlarged twice, showed the recommended look's sky mottled pink and cyan
through the clouds and its steel fringing harder than the other's, and the
recommendation reversed. Both claims had been read off a picture that could
not show them.

**Why it happens.** The pictures were rendered and opened, which satisfies
"open what you rendered" (§328) and "the pictures travel with the question"
(§359). Neither says the picture has to be large enough to show the thing the
recommendation rests on. A reduced sheet reads as evidence because it was
looked at.

**The rule.** A recommendation between renders rests on a close-up where the
candidates differ, at full size or more, opened before the recommendation is
written, and the same close-up is what goes to the owner. The overview sheet
shows where to look; it never decides.
