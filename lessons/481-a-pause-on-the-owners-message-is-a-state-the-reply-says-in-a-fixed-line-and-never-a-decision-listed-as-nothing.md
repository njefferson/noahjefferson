## 481 · A pause awaiting a go-word or the button is a state the reply says in a fixed line, and is never a decision listed as nothing

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — every reply ends with a state line in the fixed forms paused and pending what lifts it, running what, or finished, and a pause is never written as nothing open

**Smell:** a reply ends saying nothing is open for the owner while the session sits stopped on a go-word or on the button.

**Recorded 2026-11-09.** Measured on 8 and 9 November, California time.

**What happened.** Every reply's end of those two days said that nothing was open for the owner while the session was paused on the owner's go-word or on the plan's button. The dispatcher's end-of-turn reminder and rule 6 of the plans page shaped that sentence and carried no state line, and the stop guard refused a sentence naming the owner as the one a thing waits on, so the session had no accepted way to say it was paused. The owner read back through each reply to learn why the session had stopped.

**What was wrong.** A pause is a state, not a decision. Listing it as nothing open was false, and the words that would have said it truthfully were refused as blame.

**The test.** Read the last line of a reply: it names the state in one of the fixed forms, and where it is paused it names what lifts the pause. The open-for-you list holds decisions only.

**The general form.** When a gate refuses the honest wording of a state, the session reaches for a wording that is false; the gate carries the accepted words.
