## 258 · A defect you know how to fix is not a decision, and putting it in the decisions list is work handed back

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST is-this-actually-a-choice — before a closing question
goes out, answer "do I know what the right fix is?"; if yes, it is not a
question, it is a task, and it goes in the work rather than in the list. ·
CHECKLIST what-is-being-traded — a real decision names two outcomes that are
both defensible; if one option is "leave the known defect", there is no trade
and the list is padding. · JUDGEMENT — scope, cost and taste are still the
owner's, and so is anything where the record genuinely cannot answer.

**Smell:** a closing list where an item is phrased as a recommendation you
already believe. "Recommend I do X" with no alternative stated. A question whose
answer you would give the same way whoever asked. An item that would read as an
obvious bug in a report by anybody else. And the tell: the word *recommend*
followed by the fix, with nothing on the other side of it.

**2026-09-09.** A report closed with three items. One was a genuine gate — a
promote, which is the owner's by rule. The other two were: a live region on one
surface still announced-and-invisible, when the identical defect on another
surface had just been fixed three releases earlier; and a screen whose only
route to the control it points at did not work. **Both had known fixes. Both had
just been described, in the same report, as defects.** They went out as
questions anyway, twice in one session, and the instruction that came back was
to stop.

**The rules that existed did not cover it, and that is the point.** One says a
report ENDS IN DECISIONS or it ends in nothing — a list of everything
outstanding is work handed back. Another says standing practice is never a
question. Both were followed to the letter: the list was short, numbered,
answerable in one word, with a recommendation first. **The list was well-formed
and its contents were not decisions.** Formatting a task as a choice does not
make it one; it makes it a task the reader now has to hand back.

**The test that separates them.** A decision has two outcomes a reasonable
person could pick between, and the person asking cannot rank them from the
record. A defect with a known remedy has one outcome and a cost. Cost is worth
naming — "this is about an hour and a browser walk" belongs in the report — but
naming a cost is not the same as asking permission, and a fix does not become a
decision by being expensive.

**Where this bites hardest is right after good work.** Having found and fixed
seven things, the eighth and ninth feel like they need sanction, because the
session has been in a mode of reporting rather than deciding. That is the moment
to notice that nothing has changed except momentum: the same finding, met on
hour one, would simply have been fixed.
