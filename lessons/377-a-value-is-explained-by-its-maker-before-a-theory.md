## 377 · A value is explained by whoever wrote it before it is explained by a theory, and a paper's constant comes from the paper

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST explain-from-the-maker — before a record explains a number a camera, a file format or a library produced, the maker's own document is read (the camera's manual, the format's specification, the library's source), and the explanation names it · CHECKLIST constant-from-the-paper — a constant attributed to a paper is checked against the paper itself, by equation number, never against a re-implementation of it · JUDGEMENT

**Smell:** an explanation of a stored value that names a mechanism (a clamp, a ceiling, a limit) and cites only the app's own measurements; a constant credited to a paper whose source in the code comment is a notebook, a blog or a port; a matrix written out in a shader that nobody has tested on a neutral grey.

**Recorded 2026-10-01**, from an audit of Jefferson-Photography-Studio against primary sources. Three explanations had been written into its research file and decision records, measured, and acted on. Each was overturned by one document the session could have read before writing it.

**1. A camera's stored white balance, explained as a clamp.** The research file said the Z 50 cannot store an infrared white point: the gains fall outside a preset's range, the camera clamps, and two different preset slots recording one identical value proved it. The Z 50 Online Manual (Preset Manual) says a preset slot holding no measured value uses 5200 K, the same as Direct sunlight. The owner's NEF records exactly that balance, within 1.3% and 2.2% of darktable's Z 50 Direct Sunlight preset, at no edge of the range. Two empty slots agree because both fall back to the default. The conclusion that the stored value must not be developed at stands; the mechanism was invented. The manual was one page away the whole time.

**2. A paper's threshold, taken from a port.** The sky-detection code credited a "no sky" zigzag test to Shen and Wang's eq. 14 at H/4, and the record built its argument for departing from the paper on that clause refusing a frame with real sky. The paper's eq. 15 sets thresh2 = H/10, chosen in its §3.3 from a sweep. H/4 came from a public notebook that misquotes eq. 15. At H/10 the frame the argument rested on does not fire at all.

**3. A hue rotation that was a transpose.** The global hue, the mask hue and the exported camera profile applied the YIQ rotation transposed: GLSL fills a `mat3` column by column, the CPU path copied the order it read, and the result tints grey (lime at 180°) instead of rotating hue. Two records concluded from it that a global hue shift cannot serve a look, and a decision record rejected a mapping partly on a lime frame the defect drew. One test with a grey input fails the old code at every angle.

- **What these share.** Each was measured carefully and each measurement was right; what was wrong was the account of why, and the account was written from the app's own behaviour rather than from whoever made the value. A measurement of the app cannot say what a camera meant by a number or what a paper's constant is.
- **What changed.** The records and the source comments were corrected (Jefferson-Photography-Studio `9fb9eb5` and the session branch's `Internal:` commit), the hue fix carries a grey test, and the conclusions drawn through the transposed matrix are marked as owed a re-measurement rather than deleted.
