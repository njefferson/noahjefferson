## 336 · A number that will not move under the control that should move it is measuring two things

**Enforced by:** GATE Jefferson-Photography-Studio:tools/mask-truth-walk.mjs —
the acceptance walk sorts its measured population before it bounds anything,
and a `--plant-reachable` flag reproduces the old arithmetic so the split has
to be shown moving the readings.
**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST can-this-number-move — before tuning a constant to
move a figure, establish that the figure CAN move. Sweep the constant across
its whole useful range first. A figure that holds still across the sweep is not
being limited by that constant, and every round spent tuning it is spent on the
wrong question.

A photo editor's sky selection had an acceptance figure: the share of sky
covered within ten pixels of an edge, wanted at 0.85. One test frame read 52%.
The tolerance that governs how far the selection spreads by colour was swept
across 2.5, 3.25, 4 and 5 — the whole useful range, at real cost, three frames
through a browser per step.

**It read 52% at every one of them.** Spill rose from 8% to 10% while coverage
did not stir.

That was written up as a finding about the frame — that it fails in two
opposite directions at once, that 0.85 may be unreachable there, that the
honest options are a different mechanism or a different bound. All of that was
true and none of it was the point. **A figure that will not move under the one
control that should move it is usually not a fact about the subject. It is a
fact about the figure.**

## What it actually was

The denominator held two different populations. The selection spreads only
through pixels JOINED to the sky — that is the whole discriminator, and without
it colour alone readmits half of another frame — so sky seen between leaves,
whose only path to the open sky runs through branches, is beyond it at every
tolerance BY CONSTRUCTION. The figure was averaging sky the mechanism could
have taken and failed to, with sky the mechanism can never take.

Sorted apart, the same build on the same frame reads **98%**. Two of three
frames pass. The third still fails, on a real defect with a picture attached —
which is the point: splitting the denominator did not make everything green, it
isolated the one thing that was actually wrong.

## Why this is not moving the goalposts, and the test that tells them apart

The bounds did not move. 0.85 and 0.97 stand.

**Lowering a bound to meet what a build already does makes the gate vacuous** —
it can no longer catch a regression and no longer states an intention. Changing
what the bound is computed OVER keeps both, but only if the new denominator is
defensible without reference to the result. The test is whether you could have
written the definition before seeing the number. Here you could: a
connectivity-constrained selection cannot enter a region nothing connects it
to, and that was in the mechanism's own contract before any of this.

If the only argument for a new denominator is that it makes the number better,
it is the goalposts.

## The plant has to be the old arithmetic

A redefinition is checked by reproducing what it replaced. The flag that counts
every pixel as reachable gave all three frames' previous readings to the digit,
so whatever the unplanted run moved, the split moved. Without that the new
number is just a new number.

**And a plant must land in ground the thing under test cannot overwrite.** The
first plant tried in this family moved a slider one step and the test passed
with it in — correctly, because the operation under test SETS that slider. See
§335.

## The other half came from opening the pictures

The same walk reported spill of 8%, 23% and 55%, which reads as the selection
swallowing whole tree canopies. Opened, it is a large bright cloud on one frame
and the pale hazy sky above a treeline on the other two — selected correctly,
and refused by the walk's own colour key, whose saturation floor pale sky sits
under. The file's header had named that limitation for clouds; nobody had
looked at its scale. §328 is the standing rule and this is another instance of
it: the statistic had a shape, and the defect was being described in that shape.
