## 198 · A quotation can be perfectly accurate and still carry the wrong citation — and nothing inside the document can see it

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — a misattributed quotation makes every sentence around it read true, so no amount of re-reading the document finds it. · CHECKLIST cite-from-the-source — before a research claim is relied on, open the primary and search it for the quoted string; a bibliography that resolves is not the same check as a quotation that appears. · CHECKLIST both-halves — a metadata API settles journal, volume, pages and year; the CLAIM half needs the abstract or the full text, and verifying only the first and reporting "verified" checks the part nobody doubted.

**Smell:** a research note where quotations sit inside a list of several
citations, so no single source is named for any one of them. Also: "the review
reports…", "the literature finds…", where the reader cannot tell which of the
cited works said it. Also any catalogue where the citations were written by
somebody who had read the papers and the quotations were pasted later.

A design catalogue carried a graded entry quoting four phrases. Two were
verbatim in the review it named. **The other two appear in none of the five
papers the entry cites.** They are not fabricated — a full-text search located
both in a sixth paper the entry never mentions — and the claims they support are
unchanged. The attribution was simply wrong, and it had been wrong since the
entry was written.

**Nothing about the entry looked wrong.** Every sentence was true. The grades
were right. The argument the quotations supported was correct. A careful reader
re-reading the entry a hundred times would find nothing, because the defect is
not in the document; it is in the relationship between the document and a file
somewhere else.

The pass that found it also found a second: an entry naming the wrong journal
for its own primary, while **every one of its seven percentages matched that
source's results table to the digit.** Somebody had plainly read the table and
mis-recorded the container.

**Numbers.** Seven entries carrying named sources, checked against primaries.
Two defects, both in the first two entries checked, both of this shape. Five
entries came back clean, and six citations were incomplete rather than wrong.

**The transferable part is the ratio.** Defects of this class do not announce
themselves at any rate, so the only signal is having gone to the source. A
catalogue that has never been checked has an unknown error rate and reads
exactly like one that has.
