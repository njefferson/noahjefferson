## 421 · A step added to a plan after its approval, or put on its Standing line, went with no reading by the owner and no review, so the approval now records the plan's step numbers and a send outside them is refused

**Enforced by:** GATE hub:session-guards.test.mjs — the cases that approve a plan through the tool and through the owner's exit in the app and read the step numbers the marker records, amend the plan four ways and read that the record stays as approved, read the lists back through `approvedPlan`, and send steps through the dispatch gate, the review gate and the hook itself, with plants in approved-plan-guard.mjs, hook-dispatch.mjs and report.mjs for every part whose removal must fail a case

**Recorded 2026-10-23.** An edit of a plan's Steps or Order keeps the approval, by design, so that a review's ROUTE-REFUSED is answered by an amendment and not by a fresh button. The same design left two ways past the owner's reading. A step added to the Steps after approval was a step nobody had read, and the dispatch gate sent it whenever the plan's Served by line named it. A step put on the Standing line, or added there, passed the dispatch gate and the review gate both, because a Standing step is exempt from both, so that the review itself can always be sent; it went with no review of its route at all. Nothing in the marker could tell either from a step of the plan as approved, because the marker held a hash and the hash moves with every amendment.

**What is done now.** On 23 October the marker began to record, beside the plan's hash, the step numbers of the Steps section and the numbers on the Standing line (`approvedStepSet` in report.mjs), by the tool's approval and by the owner's exit in the app alike. An amendment moves the hash and the scope and leaves both lists as approved. `approvalSetProblem` in hook-dispatch.mjs is asked by the dispatch gate and by the review gate, and refuses three things. A step number that was not in the Steps at approval is refused with a sentence saying the step needs the owner's button. A step on the Standing line that was not on it at approval is refused, whether it was in the Steps then or was added after. A Standing step that is neither the review step nor a route check, named as one by the words route check in its own first line, is refused. The refusals name the rule, set no latch (the dispatch gate's carries the fence's no-latch mark, the review gate's never latched), and an edit of a recorded step's text or of the Order of the recorded steps keeps the approval and raises none of them, so a refused route is still answered without the button. `approvedPlan` hands the lists to both gates, null for a marker written before they were recorded and an empty list for a field that is not a list of whole numbers.

**The excuse is the case Doctrine §0d names.** An addition of this kind arrives with a reason, as a bypass always does: the step is small, it is the same work, the plan would otherwise stall. The reason is usually true as far as it goes, and a reason-shaped excuse is the case §0d names. The test is not whether there is a reason but whether the control was the owner's, and the plan's list of steps, and the line that exempts a step from the review, were. The gate does not weigh the reason; it reads the record.

**What the check cannot see.** A marker written before the lists were recorded holds neither and carries no check, so a plan already in force when this landed is judged as it was. The approval is of the step numbers and the four scope sections and not of each step's words: the text of a recorded step may be rewritten by an amendment, and the Standing review step's text may be edited so that it no longer names the review file by its full path, which leaves no step following it, so the review gate stops enforcing for the rest of the plan while the dispatch gate refuses that Standing step by the kind check and the other steps go by the pointer. The stop guard still counts a step added to the Order as the step left, which the dispatch gate now refuses to send, so a turn can then end only by declaring the stop in its first line. The refusal's no-latch mark travels in its text, so a refusal that lost it would latch, which is the safe direction.

**The general form.** An exemption written so that one thing can always go is a door for everything the approval is meant to hold back, and an approval that records only a hash cannot tell an amendment from an addition. Record what was read at the moment it was read, and judge every later send against that record and not against the file as it now stands.
