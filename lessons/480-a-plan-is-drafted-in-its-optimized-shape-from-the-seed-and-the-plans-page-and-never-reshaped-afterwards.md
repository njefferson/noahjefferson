## 480 · A plan is drafted in its optimized shape from the sources it is typed from, and is never reshaped afterwards

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — the shape a plan arrives in is fixed by the seed's batch print and the plans page's planning procedure, and an owner's ask for optimization, efficiency or parallel steps after the plan is typed is the sign the sources were wrong, which a session puts right in the sources

**Smell:** a plan reaches the button with its steps in one sequence and a commit and a push in each; the owner then asks for it to be made efficient and parallel, and a session reshapes it by hand at the top tier.

**Recorded 2026-11-09.** Measured on 8 and 9 November, California time.

**What happened.** The seed's batch print put every step after the one before it and landed a commit from each group step, and the plans page stated no procedure a plan is drafted by. The plan of 8 November was typed from that print and then reshaped by hand into seven file steps at once with one commit step, which halved its wall time. Four owner messages of 9 November asked for optimization, efficiency and parallel steps before the sources were changed.

**What was wrong.** The optimization was done on one plan and not in the place every later plan is typed from, so the next plan would have come out in the old shape again and been reshaped again.

**The test.** Before a plan goes up, read its Order against the steps' files: steps that share no file and no dependency run together, one commit step proves and lands once, and the cost is computed from the estimates. Where the print gave another shape, fix the print in the same plan.

**The general form.** A correction to the shape of one plan is a defect of the source that shaped it, and the fix belongs there.
