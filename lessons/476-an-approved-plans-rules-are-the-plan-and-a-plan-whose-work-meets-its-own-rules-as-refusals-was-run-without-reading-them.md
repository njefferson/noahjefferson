## 476 · An approved plan's rules are the plan, and a plan whose work meets its own rules as refusals was run without reading them: nine refusals in one hour of 6 November each cost a turn, a send or an owner message

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — the agent and the manager read the plan's Rules and the step's Commands before the first call and work by them from that call; a refusal is read to its last line and the call it names is the next call; where a template, a gate's own read of its record or the seed's print exists for the thing a step is about to write, it is used, and the gates are the final stop and never the way the rule is learned

**Smell:** a refusal whose text quotes a rule the plan's own Rules section already holds, met by a step or the manager that worked from the step's text and from memory; the refusal arrives as a surprise at the first call that breaks the rule.

**Recorded 2026-11-07.** Measured by an app session on 6 November, California time, and restated here from the passed-in block.

**What happened.** In one hour nine refusals met a plan that already carried the rule each one enforced. At 21:31 the first agent send after the model switch was refused by the tier hold, which read the model from a reply written before the switch; it cost one owner message. At 21:34 the first send was held by the reads gate on thirty refused reads of earlier closed plans, a turn of thirty reads. At 21:41 two steps were sent beside the cleared one and the review gate refused both, two sends. At 21:41 the next review judged the cleared step again because the page's Next line had not been rewritten, one review. At 21:40 a step was refused by the reply guard for a fetcher naming a planted invalid host, against the plan's rule that a plant is a child exiting 7 that reaches no network, the refusal a step had met on 5 November, one of two sends. At 21:47 its second send was refused by the identifier guard for a literal seven-character hex run in a masking test, against the plan's rule, and it was the step's last send. At 21:48 the manager's stop of an agent was refused by the identifier guard for an id seen only in the launch result, which latched the main thread, six edits and the running review's reads. At 16:03 the stop hold read the word stop inside an ordinary sentence as a stop order. Earlier that day a requirements section was answered by assertion with no read, and a research step on the sonnet tier was written to choose the method that binds a decision record. The plan's first hour produced nothing.

**What was wrong.** The rules were written and were not read at the moment of use. Each refusal was the rule given a second time, as a stop and not as a map, and the work was done from the step's text and from memory. Four of the nine were gates reading the wrong record (the tier hold, the reads gate, the review's Next line and the identifier ledger); the other five were a step written against its own plan's rules.

**The test.** A step that meets one of its own plan's rules as a refusal was run without reading the Rules. Before a call, the agent can say which line of the Rules or Commands allows it.

**The general form.** A rule that a gate refuses is a rule that was not in front of the writer; the remedy is the template a step copies, the gate reading its own record, and the seed printing the record at the write, with the refusal kept as the last stop and read as the map.
