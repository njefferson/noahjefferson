## 471 · A record of the owner's intent was written from the session's replies and checked for shape only, and four failures of 1 and 2 November came from the same place

**Enforced by:** CHECKLIST intent-from-the-messages — a record of what the owner wants is written from the owner's messages read whole, each requirement carrying the time of the message that set it, and from nothing the session said; before a plan rests on it, a line of it is opened against its message

**Recorded 2026-11-03.** Measured over 1 and 2 November, California time, in the plans of that session.

**What happened, four ways.**

- The record of the owner's intent. Section 1 of the redesign record was written from the session's own replies of 2 November. The gates that read it checked that it had the sections and the source keys, which is its shape, and none opened a line against a message of the owner. The section was replaced from the map of the owner's messages (89 of them, each with its time) in step 59.
- The afternoon's replies. On 2 November the session's replies each took their position from the newest message and not from the messages before it: nine positions in nine replies on the same question, the later one overwriting the earlier with no mention that it had moved.
- The design erased by the maker's floor. One reply, written to answer a figure from the maker's documentation, took a design that had been built from the owner's requirements down to what that figure alone would carry, and the requirements were not read again before the reply went.
- The plan's write held by the message that ordered it. A message that ordered a new plan was itself a correction by the hold's rule, so the hold refused the plan file's write in plan mode, which is the write the message asked for. It was met on 1 November and again on 2 November before the hold passed that write.

**The test.** A record of intent is opened, line by line, against the owner's messages by the step that rests on it, and the step's observation is a line found in a message with its time. A reply that changes a position names the message that moved it and the position it leaves. A figure from outside the work is set against the requirements before it changes the design. A message that orders work is not held by the rule that holds work.

**The general form.** A check of shape passes whatever the content is, so a record that answers to shape alone answers to nothing; the check that has force opens the claim against its source.
