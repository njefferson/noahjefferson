## 465 · A matter the record had settled was served as a choice fifteen times, and a recommendation that broke a standing rule went with it

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — before an open choice is written or written again, the record is read for whether it settles the matter, and a choice whose recommended option breaks a standing rule of the doctrine is not a choice; the turn a hook forces while a choice stands open is the cheapest point to withdraw it

**Smell:** a numbered list with a recommended option at the top of a reply that a stop hook, not a person, caused; the same question served again in the next forced turn word for word; a recommendation that flips between two serves with no new fact; an option that skips a step the doctrine requires (a republish of the status page at the plan's end only, a commit identity changed on a reading)

**Recorded 2026-11-02.** Measured on 31 October and 1 November. The commit identity was put up as a choice in the first reply at 16:37 California time, decided the wrong way, and then served thirteen more times between 06:37 and 07:11 the next morning, once at the top of every reply the environment's git hook forced, with the recommendation flipping to its opposite once. At 05:45 a question whether to republish the status page at every status or only at the plan's end recommended the option that breaks the doctrine's rule that the page is updated at each status. Four messages were typed to stop it and three go-words were needed to send an amended plan back through the button.

**Beside lessons 258, 438 and 450.** They say a question the record answers is not a decision, and they were written and read before this happened. What they do not say is what the open-choice-first rule does to a non-choice: it puts it first in every forced turn, so each turn repeats the error instead of being a chance to withdraw it.

**The test.** At each serving, read what the record says of the matter. If it settles it, the item is a defect or a closed matter, and it is acted on or dropped. If the recommended option would break a standing rule, the option is struck and the matter is not a choice.

**What cannot be gated.** `stop-guard.mjs` finds a choice only by its marker, and whether the record settles a matter is a reading (lesson 438). The doctrine's line beside the open-choice rule says it.

**The general form.** A rule that makes a thing prominent makes its mistakes prominent too, and the mistake is not corrected by being repeated at the top.
