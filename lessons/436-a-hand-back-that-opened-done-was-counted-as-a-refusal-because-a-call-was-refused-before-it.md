## 436 · A hand-back that opened DONE was counted as a refusal because a call of the agent was refused before it, so the status and the pointer now count it by its word

**Enforced by:** GATE hub:report.mjs — `agentEnd` and `endWords`: an end whose hand-back opens DONE, FAILED or REFUSED is a report whatever hook refusal came before it, carrying the word and the fact that a call was refused, and the status, the pointer and the steps-left check count it by that word
**Enforced by:** GATE hub:session-guards.test.mjs — the case step229f: DONE, FAILED, REFUSED and a DONE with a text after it are reports after a refusal and carry the word; a hand-back opening any other way after a refusal still reads as a refusal; the pointer counts a DONE and stays at a FAILED; and the status says the hand-back's word and that a call was refused, with the refusal printed beside it, with plants in report.mjs for every part that must fail the case

**Recorded 2026-10-29.** Measured in three plans, the last on 28 October. An agent finished its step, wrote its report, made one more call after the work, and then handed back DONE. On 28 October a Haiku agent called the session-message tool after its work, and the plan fence refused the call. The status classed the end by the last tool result before the hand-back, which was a hook refusal, and printed a finished step as ended by a refusal. The pointer, which counts a report end, stayed at the finished step. The main thread then sorted the step by hand from the report file, which is the judgement the pointer exists to remove (Q-HUB-33 from steps 64 and 69, Q-HUB-81 from steps 192 and 193, Found 226.2 and 228.23). A second send of a step that ends the same way leaves no third send, which would hold the stop guard on the plan's end though the work may stand on the evidence; that is left (Found 228.24, queued as Q-HUB-108).

**What is done now.** Two changes in report.mjs and the texts that describe them, with one case and plants that take each part out.
- `agentEnd` classes an end whose hand-back opens DONE, FAILED or REFUSED as a report, whatever hook refusal preceded it, and carries the word and `refusedBefore`. `endWords` is the one wording of an end; `endedSince`, `stepEnds`, `stepRow`, the dispatch gate's refusal, the agents block, the reads block and the pointer block use it, and the hook refusal is still printed beside the end by `agentReads`.
- `pointerOf` and `stepsReturned` count a report, so a hand-back opening DONE counts whatever call of the agent was refused before it, and a hand-back opening FAILED or REFUSED leaves the next move where it is, as before. The status line that said an end was by a refusal now says the hand-back's word and that a call of the agent was refused before it. A hand-back that opens any other way after a refusal, an interruption or a failure, still reads as a refusal. Rule 2 of the doctrine says so.

**The excuse is that the refusal did happen.** The status printed what it read, a hook refusal as the last result, and the refusal was real. What it left out was the one word the agent chose to come back with, which is the agent's own statement of how the step ended.

**What the change cannot see.** It trusts the word at the head of the hand-back. An agent whose refused call was part of its work and that opens DONE anyway is counted as done; the report file and the review judge whether the work was done. The stray call is still refused and still printed beside the end, and the cause of it, a fence refusal of a call made after the work, is untouched.

**The general form.** An agent's end is counted by what it came back as, and the events on the way are printed beside it; an event on the way to a hand-back is not a verdict on the hand-back.
