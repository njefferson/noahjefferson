## 386 · A read that answers a question already asked is reported in the turn it lands, whatever the turn was about

**Enforced by:** JUDGEMENT — before a turn that read anything ends, ask of each result whether it answers a question put to the session earlier, in this turn or in any before it, and when it does, say so in that turn's reply, whatever the read was made for

**Smell:** a read made for one purpose whose result also settles a question that was asked and left open; a reply that reports the thing the read was sent for and says nothing of what else it held; a question that has been asked more than once because the answer was never said aloud.

**Recorded 2026-10-07.** A session record was read to find which model a session ran on. The same record carried the account's ultracode flag, and that flag was the subject of a question that had been put to the session four times that morning. The read was made for the model, the reply reported the model, and the flag went unreported for a day. Nothing was hidden and nothing was missing: the result sat in the context the whole time, and a fact in a tool result is not a fact until a reply says it.

**Why it is judgement and not a gate.** A hook can see that a read happened and what it returned. It cannot see which questions are open, and whether a result answers one is a reading of the result against the conversation, which is the act this lesson asks for. A pattern fitted to this one flag would catch the flag and nothing else.

**The rule.** A read answers every question it can, not only the one it was made for. At the end of any turn that read something, go back over the results with the standing questions in mind (the ones asked more than once first) and put the answer in the reply, in that turn. The cost is one sentence. The alternative is that the same question is asked a fifth time and answered from a file that was already open.

**The general form.** The turn a fact arrives in is the cheapest turn to report it, and every later turn that holds it unsaid is a turn the asker waits through. This is the other side of the rule that a statement of state is read and never remembered (Doctrine §0e rule 15): that rule says what a reply may claim, and this one says what a read owes the replies after it.
