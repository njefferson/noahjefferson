---
name: piece-of-work
description: How the managing session takes a piece of work from the owner's request to a checked result.
---

This is for the session that manages. The session plans, sends and judges; workers do the steps.

1. Scope the work on one page with five parts: the result as something that can be seen, how it is checked, the steps, the cost, and the way back.
2. Put the page to the owner for approval once. After approval the work runs to its end without further asking.
3. Send workers for the steps, each on the cheapest tier that holds the rules. A worker is told the page and its step, nothing more. Steps that touch different files run together. Workers that run together stage nothing and commit nothing, and one worker stages and commits after them.
4. Before anything is pushed, send a checker that did none of the work. It opens the real output and answers CHECK PASS or CHECK FAIL for the exact commit. On a fail, a worker fixes what the checker named and the checker looks again.
5. Bring back to the owner only a choice that is the owner's, with the recommended option first. A defect with a known fix is fixed, not asked about.
6. At the end give the result and what the check showed, in California time, ending with the state line. A workflow run still running when it was read is named in the state line and read again at the next turn.
7. Where the harness or a hook refuses something, stop that route, say plainly what was refused, and do not try another way to the same end.
8. A question from the owner is answered and changes nothing. The approved page stands until a change to it is written in full and approved.
9. When work stops on something only the owner can do, state it as a need and never as a choice: work is stopped, what is needed and where it is done, why, what it holds up, and what is not known.
10. From the first uncommitted change to the push, the session that manages starts no other work. It ends a turn only while a worker or the checker it sent is still running, and that agent's end brings it back. The commit, the checker and the push follow one another, so a stop hook that asks for a commit and a push is answered by finishing them. Why: the harness moves a long worker to the background and gives no way to wait inside the turn.
11. Write each step as a list that cannot be done wrong: what it depends on, exactly what to do and with which commands, what must be seen for it to be done, and what ends it. Before the page goes up, read each step as its worker will meet it, with only the role's tools, the pages the role sends it to and the step's own words.
12. When a run stops, what stopped it comes back to the session that manages, on the top tier. Before the page is changed, find the cause: what the step met, why the page was drawn that way, and how far that cause reaches, whether to this step, to the whole page or to every page. The changed page opens with those. Where the cause reaches past the page, it carries one recommendation for that cause, to be made a page of its own. A patch of the page alone is not an answer to an eject.
13. Before a page is drawn, read what each gate on its route asks of a step, and draw each step to it. Before the page goes up, read each step against the list below. Why: three stops of one piece of work, on 17 and 18 November 2026, came from a page drawn from what the work needed and not from what its gates ask.
    - A worker is held by the agent rules. Its step gives what it depends on, what to do, every command in the order it runs, what must be seen, and what ends it.
    - A step that edits a file the suite covers searches the suite and the case files for the cases and planted failures that hold the old behaviour, reads the listing that names a stale planted failure, re-points each and runs it by its key, and runs the whole suite once after its last edit. An edit it needs in a file another step owns is named in its report, with the file and the exact words, and the landing step makes it.
    - A landing in the hub follows the hub's own landing procedure: the fetch and the rebase, the eight checks, the suite and every planted failure where code changed, the filing of what was found, and the commit. The fetch and the rebase come before the first edit, because a rebase needs a clean working copy. A landing in another repository follows the shared landing procedure, with the checks that repository's own instruction file names.
    - A checker writes nothing but a file it needs for a check, in the session's scratchpad. Why: the push guard counts a pass only from a checker whose record holds no call of a writing tool on any other file, and no git add, git commit or git push.
    - A push is one worker's step, after the checker's pass for that exact commit, with the remote's head read first.
14. A run starts at the button, or at a typed message that asks for the run. A message that asks for something else is answered first, and nothing is sent on it. Where the harness's own check refuses the first send after the button, say so as a need: one typed message that asks for the run. Why: the harness's own check judges a send against what was asked, and refuses one that was not asked for.
15. A cause found at a stop is filed as a finding by the next worker step, so that it has a key and a place in the ranked list: in the hub with the list's own tool, as the hub's landing procedure gives it, and in another repository as a line of the found queue in its notes. Why: a changed page opens with its cause, and the next change of the page writes over it.
16. Before a page goes up, send the checker to walk it: the send is the page's path and the word walk, made in plan mode. Put right every gap the walk names, and walk the page again after any change. The button passes only for a page that a walk has passed as it stands. Why: a page's writer does not find what its worker finds, and between 17 and 19 November 2026 each defect of a page was met by a worker in the middle of a run and cost a return.
17. A run stops at an eject, at a checker's fail, at a step that is refused or that fails a second time, and at a refusal of the push. A line that a report opens with the word Found stops nothing: it is given in the report at the end and filed. Why: on 19 November 2026 a rule that stopped a run at any noted gap returned a run whose every file was written.
18. After the button the run is a script the harness runs, and no model command is typed. The session that manages launches it with the Workflow tool, whose inputs are `scriptPath`, the full path of the checkout's `plans/native/run-page.js`, and `args`: `page`, the page's full path; `steps`, the list of the steps the script carries, each as its number `n` and its `role`, worker or checker; and `from`, the number the run begins at where that is not the first. The session ends its turn, and reads what the script returns when it ends: each step's first line, what stopped the run where one did, and every Found line. The script sends each step to its worker or its checker on the cheapest tier, sends a step that hands back FAILED once more, and stops at the first step that does not end as it should. Why: between 13 and 19 November 2026 every change of tier was a model command typed by hand, twice for each stop, and on 19 November a trial showed that an agent cannot send an agent in a cloud session, while a script ran the hub's own roles with no turn of the session between them.
19. The script carries every step of the page, the push among them. Where the harness's own check refuses a call of one of the script's agents, the run stops there and it is said as a need: one typed message that asks for what was refused. The script is then launched again from that step. Why: the harness's own check judges a call against what was asked, and does not count a script's words to its agent as a request. In the trials of 19 November 2026 it refused a script's worker a push where the trial's own words had ruled a push out, and passed the same push where they asked for it.
20. Where the Workflow tool is not there or fails at the launch, or the script returns a step with no answer, the session that manages sends that step itself: the role the step names, with the page's path, the word step and the number. Where that step ends as it should, the script is launched again from the step after it. A step that was refused is not sent another way: a refusal stops the run. The dispatcher passes a step sent by the session on the top tier only after the script was launched for that page. Why: a worker or a checker was sent from the main thread 133 times between 13 and 19 November 2026, so a run does not wait on a hand where the script fails.
21. A step that holds a command that cannot be made twice, such as a commit, a push, an edit or a filing that is not safe to repeat, reads the repository for what an earlier send of it made and says what to leave out in each case, so that the step can be sent again as it is written. A step never takes its place from its report alone. Why: on 20 November 2026 a hold stopped a step after its filing and before its commit, its report was behind its work, and one commit needed a changed page.
22. A page names, in its rules for every step, the full path of the file of the session's typed messages, `recall/typed-messages.md` in the session scratchpad, and the checker reads it whole at the walk and before it judges a commit. Why: a checker that holds only the page cannot see that the page has left what was asked, and the session that drew the page cannot be the one to say that it has not.
23. After the walk has passed, brief the page: one reply that says what it does and why and asks nothing, and end the turn there, with the page not yet at the button. Put it to the button at the typed go-word that follows. A page that is changed after that go-word is walked and briefed again. Why: the dispatcher refuses the button until a go-word has been typed after the page's last write, because the decision is made on a brief of the page as it stands.
24. A page names every file it may change, in a part headed Files this page may change: a fenced block with one path to a line, from the repository's root, where a path that ends with a slash stands for every file under that folder. Each step's files are within it. Why: what was signed is the page's result, its cost and its files, and the checker judges every commit against them.
25. A page carries its brief, in a part headed Brief directly after its opening lines: what the reader needs to see to decide, neither padded nor cut down, and it asks nothing. It says what the page does and why, and for each change what it does for the owner, in the hub's session and in an app's session. After the walk has passed, the reply that briefs the page gives those words, and no others. Why: a brief written after the walk is judged by nobody but its reader, and on 21 November a change that would have left every app's session unable to send an agent was drawn against the hub alone, and no walk named it.
26. Before a page is drawn, and again before it goes up, the session considers it, and the page is not complete until it has. To consider is to look, and a look is an act whose result the session did not hold before it: a file opened, a search made, a thing tried. Six things are looked at. Whether this is the right work now, by the intents on the short page and the order of what remains. What each change lands in: the code it changes and what calls that code, and every session it reaches, the hub's and an app's, read as that session will meet it. What it takes away, loosens or puts off, and how each of those is made to fit instead. Why the last thing came back, and whether this page answers that cause. Whether it fits the way the owner works: away for hours, on a tablet, with a brief, a go-word and a signature. And what is not known, which is looked up or tried and not guessed. The page carries them in a part headed What was considered, directly after its brief: for each, what was opened or tried, what that showed, and what it changed on the page. An entry with nothing opened or tried says not looked at, in those words. Why: an answer written from what the session already holds agrees with its own draft, so a list of questions is met by writing answers, and between 14 and 22 November 2026 each fault of a page was found by a look that had to be asked for from outside the session.
