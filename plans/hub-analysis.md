# Why work kept coming back, and the system that answers it

Written on 19 November 2026, California time, on the top tier, from the record. It is the design that the pieces after it land, one at a time. It restores no retired gate as it was, and it removes no rule.

## The causes

1. Judgement sits behind a hand. The top tier plans and judges, a tier below runs, and the only way between them was a model command typed on an idle thread. Every surprise in a run cost two commands and a button. The design of 11 November named a run with no switch of model (`plans/native-setup.md`, part A) and its means were never built. Lines 33 and 75 of the record.
2. Every change to a page is treated as a change to what was approved. The requirements name three ejects: a step to the plan, where the higher tier judges; a plan to the ranked list; the list to a person only for a choice that is a person's (lines 394 to 400). The old rules let a step be put right under a fresh review and sent only a change of scope back to the button. The rebuild lost the difference. All seven stops of 17 to 19 November were defects of a step's wording.
3. A page is first tested by its own run. Its writer was its only reader before the button. Lines 36 and 70 of the record.
4. Rules arrive at the start and not at the act. A session reads 2,065 lines at each start and after each compaction, 1,500 of them on gates that are retired. The harness's documentation puts a rule that must hold at a point at that point, and says adherence falls as an instruction file passes about 200 lines. Lines 41 to 44 and 71 of the record.
5. After a compaction a session works from its own summary. The mechanism that filed the typed messages whole and held the session to reading them was retired on 12 November, and the rule came back as text. Lines 50 and 64 of the record.
6. The requirements stand in the archive, which no session loads. A session takes its aim from the nearest message and narrows on it. Lines 68 and 73 of the record.
7. Every control is within a session's reach. GitHub holds no rule on the hub's main. Line 55 of the record, and section 11.

## Why both ways were wrong

- The way before 12 November made each rule a refusal after the act, in 154 places. A session learned a rule by breaking it, each refusal cost a turn, and many cost a typed message. It used gates to decide what is right, which line 41 of the record rules out.
- The rebuild took the refusals away and put the rules into a read at the start. It did not build what carries a run without a hand, and it lost the difference between a step and a scope, the check before the button, and the recall after a compaction.
- Neither gave a rule to the act before the act, and neither reached the top tier without a person.

## The parts

1. A run with no hand. The session that talks, plans and judges stays on the tier it is on. At the button it launches a script the harness runs, which sends each step to a worker or a checker on the cheapest tier and stops at the first step that does not end as it should. A surprise comes back to the session. No tier between them judges, and no hold ties a page to a tier. Lines 33, 34, 52, 53 and 75.

## What was ruled out

- A tier below the top that plans, with the top tier as its adviser. In both cases read on 16 November the lower tier acted on a surprise and did not ask, and nothing forces an adviser's call.
- The plan-mode alias set by variables. The harness refused the variables from a session twice, by hand it is 24 environments, and the names mislead.
- An agent that sends the steps. A cloud session does not let an agent send an agent: the harness sets the limit to one layer, and a trial on 19 November showed an agent with no tool for it.
- The top tier sending every step itself. It needs nothing new and is the fallback of part 1. It spends a top-tier turn on each hand-back.

Part 1 differs from the pairing that was ruled out in one thing: nothing below the top tier plans or judges. The script only carries the steps.

## What the trials of 19 November showed, and what was not seen

Eight short trial sessions, on the cheapest tier, in the hub's own environment. None changed a repository or sent anything to GitHub.

- An agent has no tool to send another agent.
- A checker sent from plan mode runs its command and its reads with no prompt.
- A script launched in auto mode starts at once, with no card to answer, given inline or by its file's path. In default mode the launch waits on a card.
- Its agents run as the hub's own worker and checker, on the cheapest tier, with no turn of the session between them, and hand back through the same call a worker sent by hand uses.
- The run script itself: a step that handed back FAILED was sent once more and went on, a Found line came back with its step, and a refused step stopped the run with its whole answer.
- A worker of the script wrote two tracked files of the checkout back as they stood, one of them under the agents folder, with no refusal, and ran the fetch, the rebase, the staging and the commit commands there. The commit was stopped only by the hub's own branch hook, because a trial's checkout is on no branch.
- A worker of the script pushed between two folders of the scratchpad after a checker of the script had passed the commit, and the push guard counted that pass.
- The harness's own check refused the same push in the trial whose own words had ruled a push out. It judges a call against what was asked, and does not count a script's words to its agent as a request.

Not seen: a push to GitHub by a worker of the script. It is the call that was seen, with another path.
