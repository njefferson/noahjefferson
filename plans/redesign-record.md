# The record the redesign of how sessions work rests on

Written 2 November 2026 (California time) by the research plan of that day, from sources that can be opened. It designs nothing and changes no gate, no rule and no doctrine line. The design session that follows holds the hub alone, on a fresh context, and reads this file by path.

## How to read this record

Every claim ends in a source key in square brackets. The keys:

- [T hh:mm] is a reply of the session's top-tier main thread on 2 November, read in place in the session's own transcript, with its California time. The transcript is not copied here. These replies are the session's reading of the record and of the owner's messages; they are not measurements and not the owner's decisions.
- [S20] is the audit of 1 November, the step 20 report in the session scratchpad: twenty-three findings by what each cost the owner.
- [S30] is the cost-by-event report of step 30, produced by `session-cost.mjs` (new, committed with this record).
- [S31] is the step 31 report: what the tool's maker documents, read on 2026-11-02. Every passage from it is the fetch tool's return, which is a model's account of a page and not the page.
- [S32] is the step 32 report: the hub's configuration items, read from the checkout and through the connector's read tools.
- [L nnn] is a lesson file in the hub's `lessons/` folder. [D] is `DOCTRINE.md`, [C] is `CLAUDE.md`, [N] is `NOTES.md`.

Three kinds of statement are kept apart on purpose. A count is a count by event from a transcript or a file. A quotation is a passage of documentation with its address and date. A reading is a session's account of why, and is marked as a reading. The design session should treat a reading as a hypothesis the counts and quotations can overturn.

## 1. What the owner wants

This is the session's reading of the owner's messages of 31 October to 2 November and of the owner's standing preferences, written in the record's own words and not quoted [T 07:49]. The one measured fact beside it is the volume: 66 owner messages across the four plans of the session, 44 of them not ending in a go-word, 8 of those carrying the whole word stop [S30]. The audit judged about 20 of the first 36 messages to be corrections, complaints or questions about what the session was doing [S20].

- Apps get built and the owner's part is judgement: approve a plan, read a result or a staged page, settle a choice only the owner can settle. Nothing handed back that a session can do. Nothing asked that the record already answers. Nothing asked twice [T 07:49; L 438].
- The work is made correct before a gate sees it. The rules and the app's own knowledge are applied at the moment of the act by whatever is acting. A gate is the last catch for a mistake or a bypass and is never the decider [T 06:26, 07:49].
- Domain knowledge must hold at the act. The infrared app drifted toward visible-light assumptions repeatedly and the owner had no way to hold a session on the knowledge except to force it in each time [T 07:49; section 4 below].
- Cost matters. Developing apps must not need the top subscription to pay for the layer that controls the sessions. The control layer was built to protect the owner from the tool and the owner pays for it in tokens and in messages [T 07:49; section 7].
- The owner is not to be fought and not to be hurt: no hold that reads a plain go-and-read message as a stop, no gate added each time a correction lands, no plan built from a summary, no reply shaped to pass a guard [T 07:49; S20 findings 2, 4].
- Everything is open to redesign, the owner's own preferences file and the goals block included. What is fixed is the owner's intent and the nature of the tool [T 07:49].
- Goal 6 of the goals block ("held by gates that leave it no choice to make") was read by the session first as "the owner never reads" and then corrected: it is about the main thread having no discretion where the owner's rules already decide, and says nothing against the owner reading a plan, a diff or a staged page [T 07:33].

## 4. The drift in the infrared app

This is the case of a knowledge layer that every app builds in its own session. The hub holds only the lessons about it; the app's own files were not read for this record (section 12). Every case is from the lessons.

- Lesson 319. An infrared sky defect was framed as colour noise in the first hour. Eight remedies each had a measurement of chroma behind them, so every result agreed with the frame. The defect was luminance: the bilateral filter's spatial window was left at 5 by 5 while the colour half of the same function had been widened to thirteen pixels months earlier, and the asymmetry was written in the app's own research file the whole time. What broke the frame was a kill condition written before the run, with a threshold that forbids building; it came back at 11.9 percent [L 319].
- Lesson 377. A camera's stored white balance was explained as a clamp from the app's own measurements; the camera's manual says an unmeasured preset slot falls back to a daylight value (5200 K), and the explanation was invented. A paper's threshold was taken from a notebook that misquoted it (H over 4 where the paper sets H over 10). A hue rotation was applied transposed, so it tinted grey instead of rotating hue [L 377].
- Lesson 308. A session wrote that turning off a re-balance was not the cause, wrote a second note naming a different cause, and six hours later turned the re-balance off as the fix. The fix contradicted the session's own evidence and nothing holds a later change against an earlier note [L 308].
- Lessons 301 and 302. The reporter named the cause in five words and the control that made it come and go, and the first hour went to a subsystem the report did not mention. Four instruments produced confident wrong numbers because each was built to confirm the frame; a measurement with no control beside it is a theory wearing a number [L 301, L 302].
- Lesson 331. The run that proves a test can fail is also a test of the build: a shader sampler type with no default precision made the whole shader fail to compile, and every other walk would have failed as a timeout on the welcome screen that reads as an environment problem [L 331].
- What the session read from them. The act was made from the model's prior and from the app's own output, and the reference (the manual, the paper, the repository's notes, the reporter's sentence) was read afterwards or not at all. Every remedy recorded is a checklist or a judgement, none of them adjacent to the act, which is why they did not hold [T 07:49].
- What the session proposed for that app, as a direction and not a decision: infrared facts that differ from visible light become tests with infrared fixtures; the same facts, five to ten lines, stand at the head of that app's instruction file and are restated in each step that touches processing; the review for that app reads a change against those lines; and an accepted look becomes a reference render so a change that moves it fails a test [T 07:33, 10:02].
- The scope, corrected in the session: each app's knowledge layer is that app's own work in its own session. The hub design says what every app's layer is (the shape and the checks) and no app's repository is read for the hub design [T 10:38].

## 5. The tool's documented behaviour

All passages are the fetch tool's return, read on 2026-11-02, at the address named [S31]. Pages not read, or not read in full, are listed in section 12. The harness version that wrote this session's transcript reads 2.1.316 in 2,754 of its lines (a count of lines carrying the field, read in place); the pages cite versions 2.1.283 and 2.1.304 as the first releases carrying a behaviour, and which documented behaviours apply to this session beyond that is not established.

The instruction file and memory (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory):

- "Claude treats them as context, not enforced configuration. To block an action regardless of what Claude decides, use a PreToolUse hook instead. The more specific and concise your instructions, the more consistently Claude follows them."
- "Size: target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file. Longer files consume more context and reduce adherence." The hub's `CLAUDE.md` is 1477 lines [S32], about seven times the figure.
- "If the instruction is something that must run at a specific point, such as before every commit or after each file edit, write it as a hook instead."
- "Project-root CLAUDE.md survives compaction: after /compact, Claude re-reads it from disk and re-injects it into the session."
- "A SessionStart hook that prints AGENTS.md: remove it. Once Claude reads AGENTS.md directly, the hook adds a second copy to the context." The hub's `CLAUDE.md` says its core-asks section "is printed at every start and resume by `session-brief.mjs`" [C line 24], so a loaded file is also printed. Whether that second copy matters here was not measured.
- For task-specific instructions: "use skills instead, which only load when you invoke them or when Claude determines they're relevant to your prompt."

Plan mode (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sessions, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/context-window):

- "Plan mode tells Claude to research and propose changes without making them." Approving a plan "exits plan mode and switches the session to the permission mode each approve option describes".
- On resume: "a session that ended in plan mode resumes in plan mode." This documents the behaviour lesson 432 met after the approval, for a terminal resume. The cloud page says Claude Code "resumes a session in the permission mode it was in".
- The compaction table lists "The plan Claude wrote in plan mode" as "Re-injected from disk".

Permissions and settings (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings):

- "Rules are evaluated in order: deny, then ask, then allow. The first match in that order determines the outcome". "Deny rules block in every mode, including bypassPermissions."
- Auto mode: "A separate classifier model reviews actions before they run". "In the classifier requests sent by Claude Code itself, the classifier sees user messages, tool calls other than read-only lookups such as file reads and searches, and your CLAUDE.md content. Tool results are stripped". Lesson 370 measured the classifier refusing an edit of the permissions list as self-modification.
- "Auto mode is a classifier model judging each action rather than a hard rule. ... your "never" rules belong in deny rules or hooks." (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- "permissions.allow rules in settings files do not pre-approve protected-path writes."
- Precedence: managed, then command line, then project local, then shared project, then user; and "When you set the same list key, such as permissions.allow, in more than one file, Claude Code combines the lists".
- "Claude Code reads the shared .claude/settings.json from the session's primary working directory".
- Cloud (the settings page): "Shared project settings (.claude/settings.json): read in a session with one repository". With several repositories the session "reads only the enabledPlugins and extraKnownMarketplaces keys from each repository's .claude/settings.json, not permission rules, hooks, env, or other keys." "User and project local settings ... not read." This is the documented form of what lesson 370 measured for a session launched above the repositories. It is also in tension with measurement: the main-thread refusals by the status gate and the manager fence, which only the user-level dispatcher runs, were counted in this session [S30; S32 layer B]. The user-level file here is one the container's own install wrote; the page speaks of settings kept on the person's machine. The two are not reconciled by this record.
- "Settings Warning: only individual entries fail, such as a malformed permission rule or an unknown hook event name. Claude Code skips those values and keeps the rest of the file in effect."

Hooks (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices):

- "Hooks are user-defined shell commands. Claude Code runs them at specific points in its lifecycle, which gives you deterministic control".
- "Exit code 2 is the way a hook signals "stop, don't do this."" "Exit code 1 without valid JSON output is a non-blocking error ... If your hook is meant to enforce a policy, use exit 2." "On PreToolUse, a timed-out command, http, or mcp_tool hook doesn't block the tool call".
- "Claude Code overrides a Stop hook after it blocks eight times in a row with no tool call from Claude in between." The hub's stop guard and the environment's git stop hook both refuse stops; the hub's counts are in section 7, and whether any run of refusals reached eight in a row was not measured.
- "If you keep adding emphasis to a CLAUDE.md line and Claude still misses it, that's a sign the line belongs in a hook. The reverse holds too: if you have hooks for things that are only preferences, delete them and let Claude decide."
- "Whatever the scope, keep the enforced set small. Every hook and deny rule will occasionally fire when it shouldn't, and someone has to maintain each one. If you ever find yourself at forty of them, most are probably preferences that Claude could have weighed on its own." The hub's guard files carry about 154 refusal sites across 11 files, a proxy and not a count of distinct messages [S32].
- "A prompt or agent hook asks another model to decide, so it isn't deterministic either."

Subagents (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config):

- "Each subagent starts with a fresh, isolated context window. It doesn't see your conversation history, the skills you've already invoked, or the files Claude has already read."
- "Each subagent runs in its own context window ... It also sends its own requests, which count toward the same usage limits as your main conversation."
- Model order: the per-invocation model parameter, then the definition's model, then CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL, then the main conversation's model; the aliases are "sonnet, opus, haiku, or fable". The main thread can start a subagent on a chosen tier.

- "By default, a subagent can spawn subagents of its own, up to three layers below the main conversation."
- "Give a custom subagent in .claude/agents/ only tools: Read, Grep, Glob. Now it's a reviewer that can't edit anything, and that limit is enforced by Claude Code rather than requested in a prompt." (best-practices)

Compaction and context (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/context-window, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/how-claude-code-works, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web):

## 9. The seams, the wall check and the three ejects

As the session's replies state them [T 10:12, 08:15, 08:25, 08:30]. Nothing here is approved or designed; it is the reading the design session starts from.

The ladder and the wall. Every judgement node the session had described asked whether a step can go on, which is the ladder. None asked whether the plan is still the right plan or the item still the right item, which is the wall. The wall check is a standing question with a fixed place: at every stop, at every plan boundary and at every finding, before the ladder question is asked. The three ejects, as defined moves so that nothing is finished for the sake of finishing:

- A step ejects to the plan: it stops and the plan is rewritten from the files.
- A plan ejects to the program: the plan closes unfinished, its item goes back to open with the finding attached and is re-ranked, the dependencies it moved are marked, and the next item is taken off the program. This replaces half-finishing and queueing and rewrites goal 5.
